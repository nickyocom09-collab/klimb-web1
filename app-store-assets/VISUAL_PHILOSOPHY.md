# Klimb App Store Screenshot Philosophy

The screenshots should feel like stepping beneath a freshly set wall at night: nearly black space, crisp chalk-white information, and Klimb green used as a precise signal rather than decoration. The atmosphere is focused, physical, and confident. It should immediately read as a serious climbing product while retaining the warmth of a personal logbook.

The interface is the hero. Each composition gives one product idea enough room to breathe: a complete climbing history, a frictionless logging flow, and visible progress over time. The phone surface rises from a subtle green glow, with restrained depth and large rounded geometry that echoes the app itself.

Typography carries the story in a few deliberate words. Headlines are oversized, compact, and benefit-led. Supporting copy is limited to a short rhythm of product concepts, leaving the screenshots legible at App Store thumbnail size and allowing the real data UI to provide credibility.

The sequence behaves as one visual sentence. The first image establishes memory and organization, the second shows the core action, and the third pays it off with progress. Repeated alignment, lighting, device treatment, and navigation make the set coherent without making the individual images repetitive.

Details stay truthful to Klimb: real route colors, boulder and rope grades, projects, flashes, streaks, personal bests, and the grade pyramid. The presentation is polished, but the product remains recognizable to someone opening the installed app immediately after viewing the listing.
